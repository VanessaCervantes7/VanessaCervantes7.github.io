Sociology/Administrative Studies

Although I don’t have any formal job experience, I have completed over 40 hours of volunteer work. Most of my volunteer experience consists of being a food runner and scoreboard keeper. The first volunteer experience was at Moorpark High School, where I worked the snack bar for football games and track meets. During this I would work to prepare food such as nachos, burgers, pizza, pretzels, etc. Along with this there were times where I would take the orders and give them to the people in the back of the snack bar. This taught me how to be efficient in a place where quickness is necessary. It also showed me that organizational skills are important in maintaining that fast speed and good customer service. There was also necessary communication within the work environment, between other volunteers and customers, it was important to sustain a positive atmosphere.

Another volunteer opportunity that I had was, keeping the score of youth basketball games. During this time I would maintain the official written record of the game, tracking points, files, timeouts, and player rosters. This experience taught me organizational skills because I had to keep track of the players individual points, the points as a whole, the fouls of each individual player, the fouls of the team as a whole, and coach timeouts.It also taught me how to deal with unhappy adults in an efficient and effective way without disregarding good customer service. I was also able to work on conversational skills when talking to the officials of the game, the coaches, their staff, and the players. Again it was important to keep the conversation neutral when talking to both teams in order to not make it seem as though there was bias.

Another volunteer experience that I had was preparing and serving food to customers again during little league games. Similar to working at the snack bar at the high school, the little league introduced me to new ways to take orders and run them to the back service. This community was more tightknitted, so I had to be integrated into it. One of the ways I did it was to talk and keep sure yet good conversation going with those wanting food and drinks. The fourth experience I had as a volunteer was at a marching band competition, where different high schools came to my high school in order to compete for first place. We brought the band members to and from their respective areas, from the top of the stairs to the bottom of the stadium where they would be performing. We essentially "controlled the traffic” of the area and we made sure each group had enough time to gather all of their things and go after off in a timely manner, without bumping or crashing into other schools. Maintaining contact with other event managers allowed me to get a sense of a work environment and helped build my professionalism by coordinating with the other event managers as well as the marching band participants. Having to deal with some impatient teachers or affected people in general taught me to become more patient and learn how to navigate more social aspects that I could in my career.
